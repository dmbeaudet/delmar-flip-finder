Why this one is a comp, not a flip
This is someone else's flip, already finished and stuck. The public record tells the whole story: it sold for $180,000 in December 2025, was renovated ("new kitchens, new baths, new siding and windows"), relisted at $359,000 in July 2026, and has since been cut five times down to $309,000 — with 24,000+ views and no buyer through the fall. That makes it the single most useful number on this week's board: a finished 1,800 sqft Delmar home can't clear much above $300K. Use it to sanity-check every other ARV here before you offer.
If you bought it anyway (make-ready read)
As a purchase, the heavy work is reportedly done, so this is a light make-ready, not a renovation: the obvious gap is that it has no cooling. The prudent move is an independent inspection to verify the quality behind "new kitchens/baths/siding/windows" (flipper finishes vary), a punch list, and adding A/C. The plural "kitchens" and the oversized 4-car garage are worth clarifying with the agent — confirm it's a single-family and not a converted two-unit.

What it teaches the other deals
1. Footprint is the constraint. In Delmar, finishing a 1,300–1,800 sqft home does not produce a $500K+ sale — square footage, not finish level, drives the top of the market.
2. Buy far enough below. This flipper's basis (~$180K + reno) still left no room at $300K; your purchase price has to leave a real cushion under the realistic ARV.
3. Chase sqft or expansion. The deals that can approach $600K are the larger or expandable ones (1191 Delaware, 21 Mccormack), not the small finished boxes.
